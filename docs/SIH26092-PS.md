# SIH26092: what the problem statement actually asks for

**Closed 2026-08-30.** This was the number one open item since 21 Aug, named in
`PROJECT-BRIEF-ADDENDUM.md` §6, `DECK.txt:300` and the core-design spec as the thing
everything else depended on. Harsh retrieved the official text from the SIH portal.

## The sentence that decides the pitch

> **"Smart Scheme Recommender: An AI/rule-based engine..."**

The problem statement explicitly permits a **rule-based** engine. The deterministic
architecture is not a gap to defend against the title, it is one of the two options the
ministry named. Nothing else in this document matters as much as that clause.

The theme is also **Smart Automation**, not AI/ML.

## The official text

**Background.** Socio-economic empowerment of the **Scheduled Caste** population through
concessional financial assistance **and educational loans**. Annual family income up to
**Rs 5.00 lakh**. Covers up to **90% of project or education cost** at concessional
rates, **typically 6.5% to 8% per annum**.

**Direct loan applications are not entertained.** Funds route through a **Channel Finance
System** of **over 100 Channel Partners**: State Channelizing Agencies (SCAs), Public
Sector Banks (PSBs), Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), and NBFC-MFIs.

**Challenge.** Citizens do not know which credit scheme fits them, specifically the
difference between:

| Scheme | Ceiling |
|---|---|
| Micro Finance Scheme (small projects) | up to Rs 1.40 lakh |
| Term Loan (larger projects) | up to Rs 50.00 lakh |
| Educational Loan Scheme | (education costs) |

They also cannot identify or locate the nearest authorised Channel Partner equipped for
their loan category. The result is offline confusion, **misrouted applications**, and
delays in disbursement. The ask is an **intelligent, multi-lingual** digital platform or
mobile application bridging beneficiaries and channelizing agencies.

**Expected solution, three components:**

1. **Smart Scheme Recommender.** An AI/rule-based engine taking project type, estimated
   cost, income level and **education status**, recommending the most suitable credit or
   educational loan scheme.
2. **Financial Calculator.** Dynamic EMI projection accounting for maximum loan limits,
   interest rates (**6.5% to 15%** depending on scheme) and **moratorium periods of 3 to
   12 months**.
3. **Geo-Spatial Partner Locator & Router.** Mapping integration finding the nearest
   eligible Channel Partner by user location **and the partner's current fund
   utilisation eligibility**, so applications are not sent to partners with high NPAs or
   overdues.

**Impact goals.** Financial literacy on concessional lending; transparency and efficiency
in the channel finance ecosystem; faster disbursement and better fund utilisation.

## What to do about it, in order

**All three items below are done as of 2026-09-01.** Kept as the record of why they were
prioritised in this order, not as an open list.

**1. The corpus gap is the cheapest and most damaging.** The PS names three products by
ceiling and we hold one of them. Adding the **Micro Finance Scheme** and the
**Educational Loan Scheme** from nsfdc.nic.in makes the recommender answer the question
actually asked, and it is the same work already done three times. Clauses transcribed
verbatim, `[VERIFY AT SOURCE]` on anything unconfirmed, as with every other scheme.

**2. The EMI calculator is the best value per hour left.** Pure arithmetic over values
that belong beside the clauses already held. It stays deterministic, so it costs nothing
in the trust story, and every figure it produces can carry the same citation treatment as
a verdict. A judge can type a number and watch it work.

**3. Geo-spatial partner discovery is the one to scope honestly.** SCA lists are
published; **NPA and fund-utilisation figures are very unlikely to be public**. Do not
invent them. A map of real partners with an explicit "we do not have live fund-
utilisation data, and here is why we will not guess it" is entirely consistent with this
product and far stronger than a fabricated one. This is the natural place to use the
refusal that already exists.

## What this closes

The ambiguity that could have invalidated the whole corpus is gone: **"scheme matching"
means government schemes and concessional credit, not B2B market matching.** The separate
existence of **SIH26090, "AI-Driven Market Linkage for Marginalized Artisans"**, confirms
market matching is a different problem statement.

Stand-Up India and VCF-SC are adjacent rather than central. The heart of this PS is
**NSFDC's product family and its Channel Finance System.** The lapsed-scheme catch still
demonstrates the engine's value, but the corpus should lead with NSFDC.
