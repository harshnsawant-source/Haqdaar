## What it asks for

Context given: financial inclusion for **Scheduled Caste** populations through
government-backed **concessional lending**. Beneficiaries with annual family income up to
**Rs 5.00 lakh** can access loans covering up to **90% of project cost**. The stated
problem is that applicants struggle to identify suitable schemes and to locate authorised
**Channel Partners** across banks, regional rural banks and microfinance institutions.

Three required components:

1. **Intelligent recommendation engine.** Analyses project type, budget, income and
   education, and suggests loan products from microfinance (Rs 1.40 lakh) up to term
   loans (Rs 50.00 lakh).
2. **Financial calculation tool.** A dynamic EMI calculator reflecting scheme-specific
   interest rates (**6.5% to 15%**), loan caps, and moratorium windows (**3 to 12
   months**).
3. **Location-based partner discovery.** Mapping to find nearby eligible Channel
   Partners, filtered by fund availability and NPA performance.

Stated impact: financial awareness, channel finance transparency, faster disbursement.

## Where Haqdaar stands against it

| Component | Status |
|---|---|
| 1. Recommendation engine | **Largely built.** Deterministic matching over a clause-level corpus, with proof and refusal. Stronger than asked for on correctness, and the NSFDC corpus already carries the Rs 5.00 lakh income clause the PS names. |
| 2. EMI calculator | **Not built.** Nothing in the engine computes an instalment, an interest rate or a moratorium. |
| 3. Channel Partner discovery | **Not built.** No partner list, no location handling, no NPA or fund-availability data. |

Two of the three named components do not exist.

## What this changes

**The good news is larger than it looks.** The ambiguity flagged in §6 is resolved:
"scheme matching" means **government schemes and concessional credit**, not B2B market
matching. That was the risk that would have invalidated the whole corpus. The separate
existence of **SIH26090, "AI-Driven Market Linkage ... for Marginalized Artisans"**
confirms it: the market-matching problem is a different problem statement.

The corpus is aimed correctly. NSFDC, Stand-Up India and VCF-SC are exactly this
territory.

**The gap is component 2 and component 3**, and they are not equal:

- **The EMI calculator is the cheap win.** It is pure arithmetic over values that belong
  in the corpus next to the clauses already there. It is deterministic, which means it
  costs nothing in the trust story and everything it outputs can carry the same citation
  treatment as a verdict. This is the highest value work left before the round.
- **Channel Partner discovery is expensive.** It needs a partner list, location data, and
  NPA and fund-availability figures that are not obviously public. Do not fake it. A map
  with invented branches is precisely the class of thing this project exists not to do.

**On the theme.** "Smart Automation", not "AI/ML". That is a small point in favour of
the deterministic architecture, and worth having in the pocket for the "where is the AI"
question.
